back in june, someone i didn't know got my wechat and asked me for college application advice — specifically how to include [non-trivial](https://www.non-trivial.org/) and [espr](https://espr.camp/) (which they'd just been accepted into) in their application.

i didn't feel like the right person to answer this. i barely included espr in my own application -- just a brief mention in the honors section and a harvard short essay. and more importantly, i don't even think my application was even strong as i got rejected from a lot of private university i applied to, so i was never really in a position of authority on the matter. that's basically what i told her.

but later, she asked for a coffee chat, connected on linkedin, found my discord and tried to reach out again. that's when i got annoyed and started ghosting.

— which is slightly strange, because i don't think of myself as someone who dislikes talking. i actually enjoy conversations a lot. if anything, i tend to “yap” when i feel like it. i had similar conversation with someone who have similar background and shared my insights on applying to espr and summer camps. so it made me pause and ask: what exactly changed here? why did this interaction feel different?

---

### extraction problem

part of it is probably emotional residue from the college application process. i have a strong aversion to how everything that people do are not for self-growth or fun or just the joy of helping out, but a cornerstone to college applications, and how quickly everything can be considered as something they can optimize, extract value from, or convert into resume language. being asked to reinterpret rat camp experiences[^1], through that lens felt like being pulled back into a mindset i never fully agreed with.

but that still doesn't fully explain the reaction.

i think a more precise way to describe it is that conversation is often treated as if it were pure information transfer, when in reality it is something more fragile and more conditional. on the surface, someone asks a question and someone responds. but underneath that, there is always an implicit structure, _why this exchange exists at all_, what each person is assumed to be to the other, and whether there is any shared ground beyond utility.

in some cases, information exchange is embedded in reciprocity. even if the exchange is asymmetrical in the moment, there is a sense of mutual recognition — shared context, curiosity, or the probability that roles could reverse. in those cases, answering feels less like depletion and more like continuation or investment.

but in other cases, the exchange is closer to extraction in a structural sense: one person is positioned as a source of usable content, and the other as a consumer of it. the interaction is framed by utility rather than relation. this start to feel heavier especially when the topic of information transfer is rather something that produces no positive feedback to the source.

so the discomfort wasn't really about the question itself. it was about the kind of social geometry it implied.

---

### applying this to friendship

i think this framework also maps onto friendship, though noisier.

friendship forms through many kinds of conversations — some involves info transfer, some are pure emotional support. but rationally, there are so many people and so many choices, especially when one's socializing circle is not fixed yet (i.e. if one's in college, it is super easy to meet a bunch of people just by joining a club). i think the return of a friendship can be roughly considered as

return = professional support (info transfer) + emotional support - social cost

which social cost is loosely correlating with ego (since it's harder to socialize with people with high ego, but most people don't have a high ego, so social cost are abt similar across people) — then the differentiating factor really comes down to what kind of support ur actually getting.

which makes me think: what is the point of a friendship where you can only be silly together, but not really talk abt ur futures, ur work, ur ambitions? there will be constraints that surface — assume you get something good (i.e. a summer research opportunity, a selective internship) and they don't, would you want to share it with them and hope they will be happy for your good or you will be feeling concerning abt them feeling sad for they are not as good?

i have been slowly reconnecting with people / friends i met in the olympiad training camp back in 2022, but i think most of them have become pretty different from me and i've been lowk thinking of _re-evaluating_ our friendship, of what this can bring to us, to me.

last december i felt not much socially dead because i reconnected to some of them, and i thought it's good that we can all share our college lives, even if one of my closest friends told me that they are “hella boring talking to”. i dont understand what that means, but then i noticed it myself after a few weeks — the coversation had narrowed down to: studying, their studying lives, what content they are currently learning, whether they went to the library to grind or not, etc. they still ask me how i was doing, but i think the reason is more clear, it's less of a “how u doinggggg” check-in but more of a type of 猎奇 — a kind of detached curiosity abt my intl college studying life and secretly trying to make sure we are on the same pace.

or maybe another example would be more explicit. my other friend kept asking me how it is like in the US, whether shooting and terrorist attack happen every day. i told her its not that bad but she does not even believe in me who is physically here, but rather to trust some [influencer on bilibili who vlogged herself hanging around at shady block of LA downtown and talking to homeless and people with gun](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1nQwxzhEoV). then what is the point of asking me? are u seeking for actual firsthand experience, or you have a pre-determined answer and was just hoping me to verify what you thought?

### a defense of getting ghosted by experienced poeple

based on this framework, i also think it's completely reasonable for people who are more experienced or older to ghost, and i feel super grateful for the roles in my life that are more experienced but still give me insights — ultimately, there is actually only an ε > 0 chance that it will be a double-sided info transfer, and most of the time it will just be the elder person helping out and giving out info.

i also think people should keep a higher bar, a common “rigor”, when reaching out to people to get information — please, at least get some of the information on your own, or stop asking questions that are so simple that you can simply search or ask any ai. don't ask me questions like “is tomato fried egg” legit as an english translation for 番茄炒蛋 or why my code is wrong as if im ur chatgpt and the error is simply just you didn't realize python takes input() as a string and u were assigning a thing to the 2nd character of a string — either stop completely vibing and at least know some basics, or just keep on vibing and ask ai this question because they are capable to solve this trivial question.

there is a phrase “不耻下问” — don't be ashamed to ask those below you. i claim it should be flipped. people should instead just in general 耻问, feel some shame before asking, not out of pride, but out of respect for other's time. your question should remain valuable enough to justify taking someone's attention. especially when someone's more experienced than you.

---

this is super controversial topic imo, but i think in general i'm not encouraging perfectly mutual exchanges or avoid people who reach out awkwardly. that'd be too rigid. people often reach out clumsily, and that's fine. but there's a difference between awkward and extractive, which is the thing i claim people should avoid, and in general, evaluate whether this question is solid enough to take other ppl's time.
